\section{Theory}

\textbf{v)} The normal convergence-equation in the shallow water equations is 
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (uh)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (vh)}{\partial y} = 0.
\label{eq:convergence}
\end{equation}
Normally we use that $h=H_0+\eta$, where $\eta\ll H_0$. But now we instead use a varying topography with $h=H_0+\alpha y + \eta$, where $\eta\ll H_0$ and $\alpha y \ll H_0$. Using this on \autoref{eq:convergence} we obtain
\begin{equation}
\implies
\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial t}
+
H_0
\left(
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}
+
\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}
\right)
+
\underbrace{
\alpha y
\left(
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}
+
\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}
\right) +\alpha v
}_{\text{new topographic terms}}
=0.
\end{equation}
